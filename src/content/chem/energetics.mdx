Explosives chemistry is fascinating to me, but unfortunately it tends to be misunderstood. 
Many people have heard of things like thermite, gunpowder, and TNT, 
and get the idea that these are all similar materials that go boom, 
but this is not the case at all. There are many different types of explosives, 
and they have very different properties and uses. By the end of this article,
you'll understand why thermite actually is not an explosive at all, 
why gunpowder can burn without exploding, and why 
high explosives like TNT are so much more powerful (and scary) than either of those.

## What makes an explosive?
All that's really required for an explosion to happen is for a lot of 
pressure to be contained in a small space, 
and then for that pressure to be released very quickly.
If you've ever popped a balloon, or opened a shaken soda, 
you've seen a (very very small) explosion in action.
Both of these are physical explosions,
where the gas was mechanically crammed into a small space, and then released suddenly.

[graphic]

Chemical explosives work by the same principle, but take advantage of the fact
that many chemical reactions are good at generating a lot of gas very quickly. 
If such a reaction happens in a confined space, the pressure will rise
rapidly, and if the pressure gets high enough, the container will rupture,
releasing the gas and creating an explosion. Often such reactions will also generate
heat, further increasing the pressure. This is already enough 
to understand why thermite isn't a true explosive, as we'll see in the next 
section.
